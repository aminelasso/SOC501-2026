 # SOC501-2026
Course material, datasets, and scripts for SOC501 ("Sociology: Theory and Methods") - Fall 2026. 

### Course description

This course equips students with the foundational skills to embark on any career that involves the social sciences. It has three objectives: 1) to understand the shared core principles of the social sciences, 2) use them to generate original research and 3) navigate the diversity of theoretical and empirical approaches in the social sciences.

Its central claim is that thinking like a social scientist is a skill—useful in academia and beyond. Rather than reviewing theories and methods as a list to memorize, the course is built around a small number of foundational debates. Students learn to analyze and position themselves in the foundational debates of the social sciences. The course is highly applied. Every debate is explored through concrete social objects—such as racism, economic behavior, family structures, or the causes of economic development. Instead of passively listening, students are actively engaged. Classes feature structured debates and frequent exercises where students apply what they’ve learned to solve real social science problems.


### Learning outcomes

Students will build both knowledge and skills. 

##### Knowledge

* Understand the core scientific principles shared across the social sciences.

* Grasp the main theoretical currents and methodological traditions, and the debates that organize them.

* Understand the diversity of methods and what each makes visible or obscures.

##### Skills

_Generate relevant research questions in the social science_

* Formulate a research question in the social sciences.

* Translate everyday discourse (media claims, political rhetoric, conversation) into social-science questions.

*  Apply "sociological wonder ": turning everyday experiences into social science questions


_Identify and critically evaluate social science research_

* Identify the theoretical and empirical presuppositions of an analysis, and their strengths and limits.

* Identify how differences between paradigms and methods constrain interdisciplinary work.

* Communicate across disciplinary boundaries.

_Position yourself_

* Justify the use of a paradigm or method in front of an audience.

* Apply a paradigm or method to a specific social-science object.

### Pedagogical arrangement

_General outline_

Every class, except for the first, begins with a 5-to-10-minute quiz covering the previous session's material and mandatory readings. 

The first two classes introduce core social science concepts, with hands-on exercises designed to train students in formulating research questions and translating everyday observations into social science research. Following these foundations, ten classes are dedicated to five major social science debates, illustrated with concrete social objects. Each debate spans two sessions: a first session features a lecture introducing the topic with examples and exercises, while later one a second session is a workshop built around student presentations followed by collective deliberation, from which every student writes a short report. Finally, the last session is dedicated to students presenting their final essays.

### Assessment

Your grade comes from four assessments.

**Participation, Contribution & Engagement — 10%**

This assessment covers three main sources of grades, each worth a third. First, every class except the first opens with a short quiz on the previous week's lecture and the current week's readings. Second, students will regularly do in-class exercises, some of which are graded. Finally, students are expected to keep a weekly notebook, where they note things they've noticed and start turning them into possible social science research questions.

**Continuous Assessment — 20%**

This assessment covers the final essay and its oral component. Students write a research essay on a social science question of their choice, relevant to the Moroccan context. The goal is to formulate a precise research question, identify the different paradigms and methods that have been used to tackle it, and explain which they prefer and why. The essay has two components, each worth half the grade: an oral presentation given during one of the workshops, and the written essay itself.

**Midterm Exam — 30%**

Students analyze an article by identifying and discussing the theoretical assumptions and methodological decisions made by the author(s). In-class, pen-and-paper, two hours.

**Final Exam — 40%**

Similar to the midterm: analyzing an assigned text and answering conceptual questions, but covers the whole course. In-class, pen-and-paper, three hours.
